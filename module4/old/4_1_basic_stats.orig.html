If you want to describe a dice, probability is what you use. If you have two dice, still so… but as you gather enough dice and study their collective behavior, you effectively enter the realm of statistics - the field of “many stochastic variables”. We also want to study data that does not stem from throwing dice or flipping coins. Here we will introduce you to the language used.

You have already studied statistics, but some sections here are likely still new to you. In particular, learn how to do statistical analysis using R

The Poisson distribution

Introduction: This is one of the most fundamental distributions for handling discrete counts, as we get from DNA sequencers, or from Geigen counters for radiation. It arises whenever you have a memory-less process, emitting N events/second. A common example is traffic flow although in reality it follows much more complicated patterns

  • Video of the definition and some solved examples: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jmqZG6roVqU
  • Video of some examples when a process is not Possion. In general, whenever events are correlated for some reason, this property breaks down. This turns out especially important for single-cell analysis! https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sv_KXSiorFk
