Sometimes the math just becomes too difficult, and it is better to let the computer figure things out. In this lesson, we will look at how simulations given a mathematical model can be used to analyse its behaviour. In particular, when the model has an element of randomness, we can look at the distributions of properties over repeated simulations - which in statistics is called "bootstrapping". The integrals required for Bayesian statistics (next module) are actually solved using a type of bootstrap (markov chain monte carlo), but here we will restrict ourselves to much simpler and more general cases.

We included bootstrapping in this course simply because it is such a Swiss army knife in bioinformatics. It's easy to implement, and thus ubiquitous!

Use basic bootstrap to prove the central limit theorem:

The central limit theorem states that the average of N i.i.d variables having variance   converges toward a normal distribution with variance . With bootstrap we can show also what a complex distribution it can have for small N!

  • Take inspiration from bootstrap_basics.R, located in this file
  • As for the number of bootstraps, just keep it "somewhat high". The higher it is, the more precise is your answer. Motivating the number of bootstraps is beyond this course. In practice, the number is picked based on experience. By running the same bootstrap multiple times, you can assess if the precision appears "good enough" for your problem. Tricks to increase precision include parametric bootstrapping, and bias correction. Again, both of these are outside this course, but see Deep reading at the end of this module
